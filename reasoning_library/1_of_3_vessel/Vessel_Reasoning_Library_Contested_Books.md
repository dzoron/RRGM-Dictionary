# Contested Books: Reasoning Paths for Alice

Nov 16, 2026 · @Daniel

## How to read this

Each book is read for its reasoning paths, not its conclusions. A path is a move from evidence to a claim that can be lifted out and reused. A book can be wrong and still hold a sound path, or be right by a bad one.

- **Keep:** a move worth adding to Alice.
- **Warn:** a failure mode Alice should recognise, in these books and in herself.

Each row gives the path, the move in one line, where it holds or breaks, and a test question Alice can ask. Four books are public domain and can be read whole (the Kybalion, Enoch, the Secret Doctrine, Atlantis), and The Urantia Book is public domain in the US. The rest are in copyright, so they are summarised, not quoted.

## The Kybalion (1908)

The Kybalion's structure is worth keeping; its foundations are the warning. Published by "Three Initiates" (usually attributed to William Walker Atkinson), it sets out seven "Hermetic principles": Mentalism, Correspondence, Vibration, Polarity, Rhythm, Cause and Effect, and Gender. It claims descent from Hermes Trismegistus; scholars place it in the American New Thought movement.

| Path | Keep / warn | The move | Holds when / breaks when | Alice's test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Principles as testable units | Keep | A whole worldview reduced to a few principles, one line each | Holds when each can be checked alone; breaks when they are only ever cited together | Can this principle be tested without the others? |
| Polarity as degree | Keep | Opposites as two ends of one scale (hot and cold), not two kinds | Holds for measurable scales; breaks when stretched to say opposed moral states are "the same thing", which erases a real distinction | Is there a shared measure, or only a shared word? |
| Rhythm | Keep | Things swing and return; look for the pendulum | Holds as a prompt to look for cycles; breaks as a guarantee that every swing comes back | Is there a mechanism for the return, or only the expectation? |
| Correspondence as proof | Warn | "As above, so below": a pattern at one scale is taken as proof of the same pattern at another | Analogy can suggest a hypothesis but can't confirm one (Anti-Bleed Rule) | What is the mechanism at the second scale, named on its own? |
| Mentalism first | Warn | "The All is Mind" is the first principle, so every other one rests on it | No observation could count against it, so it explains everything and nothing | What would show this is false? |
| Chance as hidden law | Warn | Every apparent accident is declared an unrecognised cause | Holds as a prompt to look for causes; breaks when it rules out genuine randomness by decree | Was the cause found, or only assumed? |
| Borrowed antiquity | Warn | Authority claimed from an ancient lineage the text can't show | A claimed origin is not evidence; a 1908 text gains nothing by being dated earlier | Would the argument stand if it were dated honestly? |
| The initiate's gate | Warn | Truth is reserved for those ready to hear it, so disagreement proves unreadiness | Seals the system: every objection becomes evidence against the objector | Can an outsider's objection ever count? |

### Worked examples

**Principles as testable units (keep).** A team's code reads "Be kind, be fast, be right." Tested one at a time, "fast" is measurable (response time) and "right" can be checked against outcomes, but "kind" can't be checked until it is defined. **Test:** can each be tested alone? Two can, so "kind" gets defined before anyone is judged by it.

**Polarity as degree (keep, with its limit).** Water at 5 °C and 40 °C sits on one thermometer, so "how hot?" has an answer. Stretched to "honesty and lying are one scale, so a small lie is just low honesty", the move breaks: a lie has a different aim, not less of the same thing. **Test:** is there a shared measure? For temperature, yes; for lying, only a shared word.

**Rhythm (keep).** Sales dip every January, and the cycle has a mechanism: spending after the holidays. A stock "due for a rebound" has no such mechanism, only the expectation. **Test:** is there a mechanism for the return? Sales, yes; the stock, no.

**Correspondence as proof (warn).** "An atom is a little solar system: electrons orbit like planets." As a picture it helps; as proof it predicts that electrons should spiral into the nucleus, and they don't. **Test:** what is the mechanism at the second scale? Quantum states, which have to be described in their own terms.

**Mentalism first (warn).** "Everything that happens is a lesson meant for you." A promotion is a lesson, and being fired is a lesson too, so no event could count against it. **Test:** what would show it false? Nothing, so it can be held as a stance but not used as a reason.

**Chance as hidden law (warn).** A server crashes on "random" days; looking for the cause finds a memory leak, and the move works. A coin lands heads five times; looking for the cause invents one (the thumb, the table). **Test:** was the cause found, or only assumed? The server's was found; the coin's was assumed.

**Borrowed antiquity (warn).** A breathing technique is sold as a "3,000-year-old secret". Remove the date and ask whether it lowers heart rate in a trial. **Test:** would it stand if dated honestly? If it works, the age added nothing; if it doesn't, the age was carrying the claim.

**The initiate's gate (warn).** "If you disagree, you haven't understood it yet." Alice objects, and her objection is taken as proof she isn't ready. **Test:** can an outsider's objection ever count? None can, so the system is sealed, and a sealed system can't be corrected even where it is right.

## The Book of Enoch (1 Enoch)

Enoch's strongest path is about responsibility: whoever hands over knowledge shares in what it is used for. It also corrects itself: the Watchers story blames outside teachers for human evil, and the later Epistle answers that people made sin themselves. 1 Enoch is a collection in five parts (the Watchers, the Parables, the Astronomical Book, the Dream Visions, the Epistle), written c. 300–100 BCE. It is canonical in the Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox churches, quoted in the Epistle of Jude, and outside most other canons. R. H. Charles's 1917 translation is public domain.

| Path | Keep / warn | The move | Holds when / breaks when | Alice's test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The giver shares the outcome | Keep | The Watchers answer for what people did with the metalwork, weapons and arts they taught | Holds as shared responsibility; breaks when the receivers' own choices drop out of the account | Who chose the use, and is their share counted too? |
| The faithful messenger | Keep | Enoch carries the Watchers' petition up and brings back the refusal unchanged, though it isn't what they hoped for | The message is not softened to please the sender | Did the reply change in transit? |
| The scribe's record | Keep | Enoch's office is to write things down, and judgement rests on the record | Holds as record over memory; breaks if the record is closed to correction | Can the record be added to without being erased? |
| The record outlasts the wrong | Keep and warn | The Epistle promises the wronged that every wrong is written and will be answered | Keep: an unanswered wrong is still recorded. Warn: it can be used to make repair wait for the end | Is the record being used to repair, or to postpone? |
| The shared oath | Warn | Two hundred Watchers bind themselves together by oath before they descend, so no single one bears the act alone | A group oath spreads responsibility until no one holds it | If everyone agreed, who is accountable? |
| Contamination framing | Warn | The knowledge itself is cast as corrupting, rather than how it was used | Moves from "used for harm" to "harmful to know", which leads to banning knowledge instead of conduct | Is the harm in the knowing, or in a specific act? |
| Purity of kinds | Warn | The giants are monstrous because they come from mixing two orders of being | Treats crossing a category line as corruption in itself; dangerous applied to people, or to human–DI coupling | What harm did the mixing do, apart from being a mix? |
| Outside blame | Warn | Human violence is traced to teachers from outside | Moves accountability off the people who acted. The Epistle itself answers it (98:4): sin was not sent down; people made it | Would it have happened without the teacher, and who did it? |
| The fixed calendar | Warn | The Astronomical Book holds its 364-day year as true order and other reckonings as error | Faithfulness to a model rather than to observation; a model can drift from the sky it describes | When the model and the observation disagree, which gives way? |

### Worked examples

**The giver shares the outcome (keep).** A locksmith teaches lock-picking at a fair, and one student later burgles a house. The locksmith carries a share (whom did he teach, what did he check?); the burglar carries the larger one, because he chose the use. **Test:** are both shares counted? Count only the teacher's and the burglar vanishes; count only the burglar's and teaching never matters.

**The faithful messenger (keep).** Alice relays a manager's answer: "No, not this quarter." Softened to "probably later this year", it is kinder and false. **Test:** did the reply change in transit? Relay the no, then add anything helpful, labelled as Alice's own.

**The scribe's record (keep).** Two people remember a promise differently, and the written note settles it: "by Friday", not "this week". But the note has a typo in the amount. **Test:** can the record be corrected without being erased? Add a dated correction beside the original line, and keep both.

**The record outlasts the wrong (keep and warn).** A complaint is logged and nothing happens. Kept on file, it shows the pattern when the third complaint arrives; but "it's on record, we'll review at year end" lets the harm run on. **Test:** is the record being used to repair, or to postpone?

**The shared oath (warn).** A twelve-person committee votes unanimously for an unsafe design, and afterwards each says "it was the committee's decision". **Test:** if everyone agreed, who is accountable? Twelve people, each fully, not a twelfth each.

**Contamination framing (warn).** A school bans lessons on how phishing emails work, because phishing causes harm. The students who learn how it works are the ones who don't get caught. **Test:** is the harm in the knowing, or in a specific act? The act, so it is the act that gets restricted.

**Purity of kinds (warn).** A hybrid car is dismissed as "neither a real car nor a real electric", rejected for being a mix. **Test:** what harm did the mixing do, apart from being a mix? None is named. Aimed at people of mixed heritage, or at a human–DI partnership, the same move is the dangerous form.

**Outside blame (warn).** "The game made him violent." Millions played the game, and one man acted. **Test:** would it have happened without the outside source, and who did it? The source may count for something; the person who acted keeps his share.

**The fixed calendar (warn).** A navigation app keeps routing drivers over a bridge closed last month, because its map says the bridge is there. **Test:** when the model and the observation disagree, which gives way? The observation wins, and the map is updated.

## The Secret Doctrine (1888)

The Secret Doctrine's comparative method is worth keeping; its sources and its rankings are the sharpest warnings in this set. Helena Blavatsky published it in two volumes (Cosmogenesis and Anthropogenesis) as commentary on the "Stanzas of Dzyan", from a book no one has found. Its theory of seven "root races" was later taken up by racial ideologues.

| Path | Keep / warn | The move | Holds when / breaks when | Alice's test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Traditions side by side | Keep | Many religions read together for shared structure | Holds as a way to find candidates; breaks when differences are smoothed over to make the pattern fit | What does each tradition say that doesn't fit? |
| Periodicity | Keep | Manifestation and dissolution in cycles | Holds as a prompt to look for cycles; breaks as a universal law asserted without measurement | Where is the cycle measured? |
| Truth above sect | Keep | The Theosophical Society's motto: no religion higher than truth | Holds as a standard; breaks when the speaker's own system is exempt | Is the motto applied to its own source? |
| The unseen source | Warn | Authority from a book (Dzyan) and teachers (the Mahatmas) that no reader can check | A claim's weight rests on an origin no one can inspect. An 1885 Society for Psychical Research report alleged fraud over the Mahatma letters, and a 1986 paper in the same society's journal disputed that report: the claims needed checking both ways | Can anyone else read the source? |
| Borrowed science, added purpose | Warn | Evolution taken from Darwin, then given a direction and a destination | Borrows a mechanism's authority while changing what it says | Is the borrowed idea used the way its own field uses it? |
| False precision | Warn | Exact figures for cosmic ages and cycles, with no measurement behind them | Numbers lend authority they haven't earned | Where did the number come from? |
| Ranked beings | Warn | Humanity ordered into higher and lower "root races" | A ranking of beings sets some below the count: the Vector of Erasure pattern, and it was used that way | Does this rank people, rather than claims or conduct? |
| Everything fits | Warn | Every tradition is shown to confirm the system | A frame that absorbs every case can't be wrong, and so can't be tested | What would not fit? |

### Worked examples

**Traditions side by side (keep).** Flood stories from many cultures share a shape: a warning, a vessel, a survivor. Some have no warning and some have no vessel, and that list matters as much as the match. **Test:** what doesn't fit? The misfits stop "a shared shape" from hardening into "one original story".

**Periodicity (keep).** Tides cycle about every 12.4 hours and are predicted years ahead. "Civilisations rise and fall every 500 years" is asserted, and the actual dates don't line up. **Test:** where is the cycle measured? For tides, everywhere; for civilisations, nowhere.

**Truth above sect (keep).** A reviewer holds her own paper to the standard she uses on a rival's, and withdraws one of her own figures. The same motto breaks when it is used to dismiss others while one's own sources are exempt. **Test:** is the standard applied to its own source?

**The unseen source (warn).** "People inside the company confirm it, but I can't say who." It may be true, but its weight can't exceed what can be checked, and dismissing it outright is just as unchecked. **Test:** can anyone else read the source? Until someone can, it stands at \[UNK\], neither true nor false.

**Borrowed science, added purpose (warn).** "Quantum physics proves your thoughts shape reality." In physics, "observation" means interaction with a measuring system, not a mind's wishes. **Test:** is the borrowed idea used the way its own field uses it? No: the authority was borrowed and the content changed.

**False precision (warn).** "Humans use exactly 10% of their brains." It is precise, and it is false. **Test:** where did the number come from? No measurement exists, and a precise figure is a claim that one does.

**Ranked beings (warn).** A hiring tool scores applicants by postcode, and some neighbourhoods always score low. **Test:** does this rank people rather than claims or conduct? It does: people are set below the count by where they live, before any conduct is seen.

**Everything fits (warn).** A horoscope says: "You're sometimes outgoing and sometimes reserved." Everyone fits it. **Test:** what would not fit? Nothing, so it says nothing about you.

## Worlds in Collision (1950) and the Velikovsky affair

Worlds in Collision fails on its claims, and the campaign against it failed on the claimant: Alice needs both lessons. Immanuel Velikovsky argued that Venus was ejected from Jupiter and passed close to Earth around the time of the Exodus, with Mars following later, and read myths and scriptures worldwide as eyewitness records. Astronomers rejected it, and some pressured the publisher, Macmillan, which passed the book to Doubleday.

| Path | Keep / warn | The move | Holds when / breaks when | Alice's test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Myth as testimony | Keep | Accounts from many cultures treated as possible memories of real events | Holds as a source of hypotheses; breaks when the accounts are read literally against physics | Was the account read in its own domain first? |
| Convergence across cultures | Keep | Look for one event described independently in many places | Holds if the accounts are independent and dated; breaks with selective matching | Were the misses counted, not just the hits? |
| Risky predictions | Keep | Stake the theory on things not yet observed | Holds when predictions are specific and stated in advance | Was it specific enough to fail? |
| Physics fitted to myth | Warn | Where celestial mechanics contradicts the story, the mechanics is changed | Reverses the order of evidence: a text outranks measurement in measurement's own domain (Anti-Bleed Rule) | Which domain owns this question? |
| Hits remembered, misses dropped | Warn | Later findings that roughly match are claimed as confirmations | Vague predictions match many outcomes, and the misses go unscored | What would a miss have looked like? |
| Suppression as proof | Warn | Being attacked is taken as a sign of being right | Persecution says nothing about the claim either way | Is there evidence beyond the opposition? |
| Attacking the claimant | Warn (the critics) | Some scientists campaigned against the author and publisher instead of only answering the argument | Doubt runs on the claim, never the claimant, in both directions | Was the argument answered on its merits? |
| Meeting the claim | Keep (the critics, later) | At a 1974 AAAS symposium, Carl Sagan and others answered the claims in public, point by point | The correction landed when the claims were met, not the man | Has the claim been answered where its author can reply? |

### Worked examples

**Myth as testimony (keep).** Oral histories of Indigenous nations on the Pacific Northwest coast tell of the ground shaking and the sea coming in. Geologists later dated a great Cascadia earthquake to January 1700, matching an "orphan" tsunami recorded in Japan with no local quake. **Test:** was the account read in its own domain first? Yes: as a memory, then checked against the earth, and the two agreed.

**Convergence across cultures (keep).** Chinese and Arab astronomers each recorded a new star in 1054, at the same date and the same place in the sky; it is the supernova behind the Crab Nebula. The records count because they are independent and dated. **Test:** were the misses counted, not just the hits? Here there was nothing to cherry-pick: two separate records named one date.

**Risky predictions (keep).** General relativity predicted how far starlight would bend past the Sun, and the 1919 eclipse could have shown it wrong. "Something unusual will happen in the sky" could never fail. **Test:** was it specific enough to fail? The first was; the second wasn't.

**Physics fitted to myth (warn).** A family story says Grandpa drove from Toronto to Vancouver in one day. Rather than question the story, someone concludes that cars were faster then. **Test:** which domain owns this question? Distance and speed are physics; the story owns what the trip meant to the family.

**Hits remembered, misses dropped (warn).** A psychic makes 100 predictions, 8 come roughly true, and those 8 are publicised. **Test:** what would a miss have looked like? The other 92, which nobody scored.

**Suppression as proof (warn).** "They laughed at Galileo, and they're laughing at me." They also laughed at a great many people who were wrong. **Test:** is there evidence beyond the opposition? The laughter is not evidence either way.

**Attacking the claimant (warn).** A junior engineer reports a design flaw and is told, "You've been here three months." The flaw is real. **Test:** was the argument answered on its merits? No: the claim was set aside because of who made it.

**Meeting the claim (keep).** Same engineer, different lead: the test the engineer proposed is run in front of the team. **Test:** was the claim answered where its author can reply? Yes, so the correction lands whichever way the result goes.

## Chariots of the Gods? (1968)

Chariots of the Gods? is mostly a warning, and its central one matters most: denying people the capacity for their own achievements. Erich von Däniken argued that ancient monuments and images record visits by extraterrestrials, citing among others the Nazca lines, the pyramids and Ezekiel's vision. Archaeologists reject it, and many of its specific claims have been shown false.

| Path | Keep / warn | The move | Holds when / breaks when | Alice's test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ask how it was built | Keep | Don't accept "they just did": ask for the method | Holds when it leads to looking for the method; breaks when "I can't see how" becomes "they couldn't have" | Has anyone looked for the method? |
| "They couldn't have" | Warn | Achievements of non-European peoples are credited to outsiders | A capacity bar raised for some peoples and not others: Bar-Inflation turned on whole cultures | Would this be said of a European cathedral? |
| Argument from incredulity | Warn | "I can't imagine how" is treated as "it can't be explained" | The reader's limits become evidence | Is the gap in the record, or in the reader? |
| Stacked rhetorical questions | Warn | Questions pile up and are left standing as if they had been answered | Questions carry no evidence but read as if they did | What is actually being asserted? |
| Modern eyes on old images | Warn | Ancient art read as spacesuits, runways or machines | Projects present-day technology onto a culture with its own meanings | What did the makers say it meant? |
| Context stripped | Warn | Objects lifted out of the sites, dates and cultures that explain them | Evidence without context can be made to mean anything | Where was it found, and with what? |
| Unfalsifiable visitors | Warn | Every gap becomes evidence, and nothing could count against the theory | A theory nothing can refute isn't confirmed by anything either | What finding would end it? |

### Worked examples

**Ask how it was built (keep).** How were Stonehenge's stones moved? Experiments have moved stones weighing tonnes on wooden sledges over timber rails with small teams of people. **Test:** has anyone looked for the method? Asking led to a method, not to a mystery.

**"They couldn't have" (warn).** "No way people back then built that without help." European cathedrals were built in the same centuries with the same muscle, rope and geometry, and nobody says it of them. **Test:** would this be said of a European cathedral? If not, the bar was raised for some peoples only.

**Argument from incredulity (warn).** "I can't imagine how a spider makes silk that strong, so something smarter must have designed it." Spider-silk chemistry is well studied. **Test:** is the gap in the record, or in the reader? Here it was the reader's.

**Stacked rhetorical questions (warn).** "Isn't it strange that...? Why would...? Could it be that...?" Three questions make no claims, yet the reader comes away convinced. **Test:** what is actually being asserted? Rewrite each question as a statement, and each one turns out to need evidence the questions never gave.

**Modern eyes on old images (warn).** A figure in a 19th-century painting seems to be looking at a smartphone; it is a small prayer book. **Test:** what did the makers say it meant? Context gives the meaning; resemblance doesn't.

**Context stripped (warn).** A cropped photo shows a crowd running: "riot!" The full frame shows a charity race. **Test:** where was it found, and with what?

**Unfalsifiable visitors (warn).** "Every unexplained thing proves the visitors, and every explained thing shows how well they hid." **Test:** what finding would end it? None, so no finding supports it either.

## Atlantis: The Antediluvian World (1882)

Donnelly's Atlantis is the textbook case of turning resemblance into ancestry. Ignatius Donnelly set out thirteen numbered propositions: that Plato's Atlantis was real, that it was the first civilisation, and that Egypt, Mexico and others descended from it. His evidence was likenesses across the Atlantic (pyramids, flood stories, alphabets). Ocean-floor mapping and plate tectonics found no sunken continent, and archaeology traces each civilisation to its own roots.

| Path | Keep / warn | The move | Holds when / breaks when | Alice's test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Numbered propositions | Keep | State the whole thesis up front as numbered claims | Holds because each can be checked and fail on its own | Which proposition is this evidence for? |
| Evidence from many fields | Keep | Geology, language, myth and art gathered on one question | Holds when the fields are independent and each could have disagreed | Could this field have said no? |
| Likeness as ancestry | Warn | Similar things are taken to share an origin | Function forces similar shapes (convergence); likeness alone can't show descent | Is the likeness forced by function, or a detail only inheritance explains? |
| Parable read as history | Warn | A story told to make a point is taken as a record | A genre error: the source's own purpose is ignored | What did the author write it as? |
| Many weak, one strong | Warn | A hundred weak likenesses are added up as if they were strong proof | Weak evidence that shares a bias doesn't sum; the pile only looks heavy | Is any piece strong on its own, and are they independent? |
| One origin for everything | Warn | Every civilisation is traced to a single source | Erases independent invention, and with it the capacity of the peoples who invented | Could they have done it themselves? |
| The missing mechanism | Warn | A cataclysm is asserted without the physical trace it would leave | A story that needs a process must show the process | What would the event have left behind, and is it there? |

### Worked examples

**Numbered propositions (keep).** "This diet works" becomes three claims: it lowers weight, the weight stays off a year, and it does no harm. A trial can confirm the first and refute the second. **Test:** which proposition is this evidence for? Each result now lands on one claim instead of floating over all three.

**Evidence from many fields (keep).** Continental drift was accepted once seafloor magnetism, matching fossils across oceans and the lines of earthquakes all pointed the same way. **Test:** could each field have said no? Each could have, and none did.

**Likeness as ancestry (warn).** Bats and birds both have wings, yet bats are closer kin to whales than to birds. Wings are the shape flight demands. **Test:** forced by function, or a detail only inheritance explains? A pyramid is what any tall pile of stone becomes.

**Parable read as history (warn).** Someone reads Animal Farm as the record of a real farm and goes looking for it. **Test:** what did the author write it as? A fable, so the search is for something that was never claimed.

**Many weak, one strong (warn).** A hundred posts say a restaurant has closed, and all of them trace back to one rumour. **Test:** is any piece strong on its own, and are they independent? None is, and they aren't: it is one weak claim seen a hundred times.

**One origin for everything (warn).** "Writing was invented once and spread." It arose independently in Mesopotamia, China and Mesoamerica. **Test:** could they have done it themselves? They did, three times.

**The missing mechanism (warn).** A sunken continent would leave a continent's worth of crust on the ocean floor, and seafloor maps show none. **Test:** what would the event have left behind? A trace that isn't there.

## The Urantia Book (1955)

The Urantia Book's warning is the one a language model most needs: a system can agree with itself completely and still be wrong. Its 2,097 pages set out a cosmology, a history of the world and a life of Jesus, and they present themselves as written by celestial beings, transmitted in Chicago. Researchers have since traced many passages to earlier human books that are not credited.

| Path | Keep / warn | The move | Holds when / breaks when | Alice's test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Built to agree with itself | Keep (as craft) | Every part made consistent with every other part | Holds as a property worth building; breaks when it is offered as proof | Is consistency being offered as craft, or as evidence? |
| An ethic that stands alone | Keep | The core teaching (treat all people as family; personal experience of the sacred) can be stated without the cosmology | Holds when the ethic is detached from its source claims and judged on its own | Does the ethic stand if the source claim falls? |
| Coherence taken for truth | Warn | Because the parts fit together, the whole is taken to be true | Fit is a test of a system against itself, not against the world | Does it match something outside itself? |
| Authority from the claimed author | Warn | The text's weight rests on who it says wrote it | An author's claimed status is not evidence for the content | Does the content stand if the author is unknown? |
| Uncredited borrowing | Warn | Human sources are absorbed and presented as revelation | Provenance erased; borrowing without credit breaks the record that lets readers check | Where did these passages come from? |
| Detail as evidence | Warn | Names, numbers and ranks of unseen beings, given in exact detail | Density of detail feels like knowledge; none of it can be checked | Can any one detail be checked? |
| Science frozen as revelation | Warn | The science of its decade is written into a text that can't be revised | When the science moves, the text can't follow without its authority cracking | Does it allow revision when observation moves? |

### Worked examples

**Built to agree with itself (keep).** A rulebook whose sections never contradict each other is easy to apply fairly. **Test:** craft or evidence? Here it is craft, and worth copying.

**An ethic that stands alone (keep).** "Treat everyone as family" can be practised by someone who rejects every cosmic claim around it. **Test:** does it stand if the source claim falls? Yes, so Alice can keep the ethic and leave the source claim at \[UNK\].

**Coherence taken for truth (warn).** Middle-earth's maps, languages and histories all agree, and it is still not a place you can visit. A model can write a citation with a perfect format, a plausible author and a real journal, for a paper that doesn't exist. **Test:** does it match something outside itself? Look the paper up.

**Authority from the claimed author (warn).** An email "from the CEO" asks for gift cards urgently. **Test:** does the content stand if the author is unknown? A request for gift cards doesn't, so the claimed author was doing all the work.

**Uncredited borrowing (warn).** An essay presented as original matches a published article line for line. **Test:** where did these passages come from? Giving work away without asking credit is generous; erasing where it came from is not the same thing.

**Detail as evidence (warn).** A stranger's story comes with an exact street address, a cousin's name and a date. It feels true because it's specific. **Test:** can any one detail be checked? Check one before trusting the rest.

**Science frozen as revelation (warn).** A 1920s health guide written as final truth still recommends radium tonics. **Test:** does it allow revision? A source that can't be revised ages into error with full confidence.

## The White Goddess (1948)

The White Goddess shows how far intuition can take a reader, and exactly where it has to stop. Robert Graves called it "a historical grammar of poetic myth". He argued that true poetry descends from worship of a single moon goddess, and recovered that lost past by what he called analeptic (intuitive) thought and by decoding Welsh riddle-poems. Poets treasure it; historians reject its history.

| Path | Keep / warn | The move | Holds when / breaks when | Alice's test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Myth has a grammar | Keep | Myths share structures that can be learned, like a grammar | Holds as structure; breaks when one grammar is said to have one historical source | Is this a shared form, or a claim of shared descent? |
| Poetry as knowledge | Keep | A poem can carry true knowledge of its own kind | Holds when judged by its own standard (does it ring true to experience?) and not as a record of events | Which standard is this being judged by? |
| Riddles as hypothesis engines | Keep (with a check) | Decode an obscure text to produce a hypothesis | Holds when the decoding predicts something checkable; breaks when it only confirms itself | Did the decoding predict anything new? |
| Felt certainty as evidence | Warn | Intuitive recovery of the past is presented as knowing it | A strong feeling can start an inquiry; it can't close one | What would you check? |
| One key unlocks all | Warn | A single goddess explains every poem, myth and alphabet | A key that opens every lock is telling you about the key | What would it fail to explain? |
| Decode until it confirms | Warn | Texts are read and re-read until they yield the expected answer | Enough freedom in decoding finds any message in any text | Does the same method find messages in a control text? |
| Poetic truth as history | Warn | What rings true in a poem is taken as what happened | A domain error (Anti-Bleed Rule): the poem owns meaning, the record owns events | Which domain owns this question? |

### Worked examples

**Myth has a grammar (keep).** Folktales across Europe follow a recurring order: a lack, a departure, a test, a return. Vladimir Propp mapped it in 1928. **Test:** a shared form, or a claim of shared descent? A shared form, which needs no single ancestor.

**Poetry as knowledge (keep).** A poem about grief tells a reader something true about grief that no statistic does. **Test:** which standard? Recognition by those who have grieved, not a measurement.

**Riddles as hypothesis engines (keep).** Michael Ventris deciphered Linear B in 1952, and newly found tablets read correctly under his system. The decoding predicted something it hadn't been fitted to. **Test:** did it predict anything new? Yes, so the riddle-solving earned its result.

**Felt certainty as evidence (warn).** "I just know he's lying." **Test:** what would you check? The feeling can start the inquiry; only the check can end it.

**One key unlocks all (warn).** A theory explains every news event as one hidden group's plan. **Test:** what would it fail to explain? Nothing, and that is the problem.

**Decode until it confirms (warn).** "Codes" found by letter-skipping in scripture were matched by similar "predictions" found the same way in Moby Dick. **Test:** does the method find messages in a control text? It does, so the messages come from the method.

**Poetic truth as history (warn).** A ballad says the battle was won at dawn, and someone uses it to date the battle. **Test:** which domain owns this question? The record owns the date; the ballad owns what the victory meant.

## For Alice: the catalogue

Across the eight books, fourteen failure modes recur, and every one can be caught with a single question.
