## C2-N-0001 | national

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Relating to a nation as a whole, or belonging to a particular country; also a citizen of a country.

**Example:** The national election is in the spring; she is a French national.

**RRGM bridge:** National sets the scale at the whole nation, above local or regional and below international.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Of or belonging to a nation as a whole.

**Active domains:** [REL], [ECO]

**Domain entries:**

- [REL] National identity binds people through shared history, language or citizenship, and can include or exclude.
- [ECO] National economies, budgets and statistics are measured at the country level.

**Count condition:** Something that concerns, belongs to or is organised at the level of a nation.

**Specific transfer boundary:** National does not mean uniform: a national policy can land very differently in different regions. "National" in a name ("National Bank") may be branding rather than state ownership.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-N-0002 | news

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Newly received information about recent events, especially as reported by newspapers, broadcasters or websites.

**Example:** Have you heard the news? The bridge reopens Monday.

**RRGM bridge:** News is the report of a change in the world, carried to people who were not there.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A report of recent events, passed on to those who did not witness them.

**Active domains:** [REL], [DIG], [FOR]

**Domain entries:**

- [REL] News shapes what a community knows and talks about; choices about what to report are choices about attention.
- [DIG] Online news spreads quickly and is filtered by feeds and algorithms, which can favour engagement over accuracy.
- [FOR] A news report is a claim with a source; its reliability depends on the source and on checking.

**Count condition:** Recent information reported to people who did not observe it directly.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Being in the news does not make something important or true, and absence from the news does not mean it didn't happen. Report and event are separate: the report can be wrong.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-N-0003 | nice

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Pleasant, agreeable or kind; also (of a distinction) fine or subtle.

**Example:** It was nice of you to wait; that's a nice point.

**RRGM bridge:** Nice names mild positive value, an easy approval that asks little.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Pleasantly agreeable, or finely precise.

**Active domains:** [AFF], [REL], [LIN]

**Domain entries:**

- [AFF] A mild positive response to something pleasant. Provisional domain.
- [REL] Being nice smooths interaction; it can differ from being kind, which may sometimes mean saying something unwelcome.
- [LIN] Its meaning has shifted greatly over centuries, from "foolish" to "precise" to "pleasant".

**Count condition:** Something or someone judged pleasant, kind or finely drawn.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Nice is not the same as good or honest; politeness can hide disagreement. "A nice distinction" means a subtle one, not a pleasant one.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]; [AFF] PROVISIONAL

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1
