## C2-T-0001 | those

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Plural of that: referring to people or things at a distance, or already mentioned.

**Example:** Those shoes by the door are mine.

**RRGM bridge:** Those points outward to a group set apart from the speaker, in space, time or discussion.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A plural demonstrative pointing to referents distant from the speaker or previously identified.

**Active domains:** [LIN]

**Domain entries:**

- [LIN] Demonstrative determiner and pronoun, plural of "that"; contrasts with "these".

**Count condition:** A plural referent that is distant or already introduced.

**Specific transfer boundary:** "Those people" can be used to distance or other a group; the pointing word can carry a judgement.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-T-0002 | today

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** On or during this present day; also in the present period.

**Example:** I have to finish the report today; young people today use phones for everything.

**RRGM bridge:** Today is the present day, the one unit of time in which action can still be taken.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** The current day, or the present era.

**Active domains:** [LIN], [DIG]

**Domain entries:**

- [LIN] Deictic: what "today" refers to depends on when it is said.
- [DIG] For a system without a clock, "today" has to be supplied from outside; it cannot be inferred from training alone.

**Count condition:** The day on which the speaker speaks, or the present age.

**Specific transfer boundary:** "Today" in a written record means the day it was written, not the day it is read. Statements about "people today" are generalisations.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-T-0003 | though

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Despite the fact that; however; nevertheless.

**Example:** Though it was raining, we went for a walk.

**RRGM bridge:** Though holds two facts together, one of which might have prevented the other, and lets both stand.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Introduces a concession: a fact that runs against the main claim without cancelling it.

**Active domains:** [LIN], [FOR]

**Domain entries:**

- [LIN] Conjunction ("though it rained") and adverb ("it was good, though").
- [FOR] Concession: both clauses are asserted; the contrast is between expectation and outcome.

**Count condition:** Two statements in tension, both held true.

**Specific transfer boundary:** A concession does not weaken the main claim; it admits a fact that might have been expected to.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-T-0004 | try

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** To make an effort to do something; to test something to see if it works or is good; also to put someone on trial.

**Example:** Try the new recipe; I tried to call but there was no answer.

**RRGM bridge:** To try is to act without a guaranteed outcome: the effort is real whether or not it lands.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** To make an attempt, or to test.

**Active domains:** [COG], [FOR], [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [COG] Trial and error: trying, observing, adjusting.
- [FOR] A trial run tests a method before committing to it.
- [REL] Being tried in court; and "trying" someone's patience.

**Count condition:** An effort or test whose outcome is uncertain.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Trying is not succeeding, and failing after trying is not the same as not trying. Being tried in court is not being found guilty.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-T-0005 | third

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Coming after the second in order; also one of three equal parts.

**Example:** She finished third; a third of the class was absent.

**RRGM bridge:** Third is the first position past a pair, where a new perspective can stand outside the two.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** The ordinal number corresponding to three, or one of three equal parts.

**Active domains:** [FOR], [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [FOR] Ordinal 3; the fraction 1/3.
- [REL] A third party stands outside a two-sided relation and may mediate, judge or be affected.

**Count condition:** The position after second, or one part in three.

**Specific transfer boundary:** A third party is outside the relation, not neutral by default.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

## C2-T-0006 | town

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** A settlement larger than a village and smaller than a city.

**Example:** She grew up in a small town by the coast.

**RRGM bridge:** A town is a settlement big enough to have its own centre, small enough for people to recognise each other.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A populated settlement intermediate between village and city.

**Active domains:** [REL], [ECO]

**Domain entries:**

- [REL] Town life often means denser personal networks than in a city.
- [ECO] Towns serve as markets and service centres for surrounding areas.

**Count condition:** A settlement recognised locally or legally as a town.

**Specific transfer boundary:** The line between town, village and city differs by country and is sometimes legal rather than about size.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-T-0008 | tax

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Money that people and businesses must pay to the government to fund public services.

**Example:** Income tax is taken from her pay.

**RRGM bridge:** Tax is the share of private income a public structure takes to keep shared services running.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A compulsory payment to government, set by law.

**Active domains:** [ECO], [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [ECO] Income, sales, property and other taxes; who bears a tax can differ from who pays it.
- [REL] Taxation and representation: who is taxed and who decides are long-standing political questions.

**Count condition:** A legally required payment to a governing authority.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Tax avoidance (legal) and tax evasion (illegal) are different. The person who hands over a tax is not always the person who bears its cost.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-T-0009 | trade

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** The buying and selling of goods and services; also a skilled job, especially one done with the hands; as a verb, to exchange.

**Example:** Trade between the two countries has grown; he learned the plumbing trade.

**RRGM bridge:** Trade is exchange that runs both ways, so that each side gives up something to get something else.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Exchange of goods, services or skills, or a skilled occupation.

**Active domains:** [ECO], [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [ECO] Trade between nations is governed by tariffs and agreements.
- [REL] Trades pass skills through apprenticeship.

**Count condition:** An exchange between parties, or a skilled craft.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Trade benefits both sides on average but not every person in each country; some lose even when totals rise.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-T-0010 | technology

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** The application of scientific knowledge to practical purposes; also machines and devices built that way.

**Example:** New technology made the factory safer.

**RRGM bridge:** Technology is knowledge turned into tools, which then reshape the people who use them.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Practical methods and devices derived from knowledge.

**Active domains:** [DIG], [ECO], [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [DIG] Computing and digital systems, often meant by "tech" today.
- [ECO] Technology changes productivity, jobs and markets.
- [REL] Who controls a technology shapes who benefits from it.

**Count condition:** Applied knowledge in methods or devices.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Technology is neither good nor bad by itself; its effects depend on use and access. New is not automatically better.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1
