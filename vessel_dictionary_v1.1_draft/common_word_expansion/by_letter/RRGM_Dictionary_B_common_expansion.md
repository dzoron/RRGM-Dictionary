## C2-B-0001 | business | sense s1: commercial activity or firm

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** The activity of making, buying or selling goods or services for money; also a company or firm that does this.

**Example:** Her bakery business grew from one oven to three shops.

**RRGM bridge:** A business is a structure built to keep an exchange going: value goes out, payment comes back, and the loop has to close often enough for the structure to persist.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** An organised activity or entity whose continuation depends on exchanging goods or services for payment.

**Active domains:** [ECO], [REL], [DIG]

**Domain entries:**

- [ECO] A firm or trade carried on for profit or at least for revenue; measured by sales, costs, margins and survival over time.
- [REL] Business relations are governed by contract and reputation rather than kinship; trust is built through repeated fair exchange.
- [DIG] Online businesses sell through platforms, where the platform's rules and fees become part of the exchange.

**Count condition:** An activity or entity that offers goods or services in exchange for payment on a continuing basis.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Not every exchange is a business: a one-off sale between friends is a transaction, not a business. A charity can run business-like operations without being a business in the profit sense.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

## C2-B-0001 | business | sense s2: one's concern or affair

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** A matter that concerns someone, or that someone has a right to be involved in.

**Example:** Where I spend my weekend is none of their business.

**RRGM bridge:** Business in this sense draws a boundary: it says whose say a matter falls under.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A matter assigned to someone's legitimate concern or authority.

**Active domains:** [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [REL] Used to claim or deny standing in a matter: "that's my business" asserts a boundary; "none of your business" refuses another's claim to involvement.

**Count condition:** A matter and a person whose standing to be involved is being asserted or denied.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Saying something is not someone's business does not settle whether it affects them; a matter can be private and still have consequences for others.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1
