## C2-H-0001 | his

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Belonging to or associated with a male person or animal already mentioned; possessive form of he.

**Example:** He left his keys on the table.

**RRGM bridge:** His ties a thing to a person referred to as he; the possession or association is carried by the pronoun.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Possessive marking association with a referent identified as he.

**Active domains:** [LIN], [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [LIN] A possessive determiner and pronoun ("his car"; "the car is his"); formerly also used generically for anyone, now often replaced by "their".
- [REL] Pronoun use follows how a person names themselves; see he.

**Count condition:** A thing associated with a referent already identified as he.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Possession in grammar is not always ownership: "his train" or "his mother" marks association, not property.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-H-0002 | her

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Object form of she ("I saw her"), and possessive meaning belonging to or associated with her ("her coat").

**Example:** I gave her the book; it was her idea.

**RRGM bridge:** Her points to a person referred to as she, either as the one acted on or as the one a thing belongs with.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Object and possessive form of a referent identified as she.

**Active domains:** [LIN], [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [LIN] Serves as both object pronoun and possessive determiner; "hers" is the standalone possessive.
- [REL] Pronoun use follows how a person names themselves; see she. Ships, nations and machines are sometimes called "her" by convention.

**Count condition:** A referent identified as she, as object or possessor.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Calling a ship or machine "her" is a convention of speech, not a claim about the thing's nature. As with his, possessive marks association, not always ownership.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-H-0003 | however

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Nevertheless; in spite of that; or in whatever way or to whatever degree.

**Example:** The plan was sound; however, nobody had the time to run it.

**RRGM bridge:** However turns the line of argument: it keeps what came before and adds a weight on the other side.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Introduces a contrast that qualifies, without cancelling, what preceded it; or means "in whatever manner".

**Active domains:** [LIN], [FOR]

**Domain entries:**

- [LIN] A contrastive connective, usually set off by punctuation; also an adverb of manner or degree ("however hard she tried").
- [FOR] Marks a qualification: the earlier claim stands but its scope or force is limited.

**Count condition:** A preceding statement and a following point that runs against or limits it.

**Specific transfer boundary:** However does not refute what came before; it limits it. A "however" clause still needs its own support.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-H-0004 | hot

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Having a high temperature; also spicy; also (informally) popular, attractive, or intense.

**Example:** The soup is hot; this year's hot toy sold out.

**RRGM bridge:** Hot names high heat, and by extension strong intensity of taste, demand or attention.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Of high temperature or intensity.

**Active domains:** [PHY], [BIO], [AFF]

**Domain entries:**

- [PHY] High thermal energy; heat flows from hot to cold.
- [BIO] Spicy heat is a pain response triggered by compounds like capsaicin, not by temperature.
- [AFF] "Hot-headed" or "hot-tempered" describes quick anger. Provisional domain.

**Count condition:** Something of high temperature, sharp spiciness, or intense demand or feeling.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Spicy "hot" is a sensation, not heat. "Hot" as popular says nothing about quality.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]; [AFF] PROVISIONAL

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-H-0005 | hate

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** To feel intense dislike for someone or something; also that feeling.

**Example:** I hate waiting in queues.

**RRGM bridge:** Hate is a sustained turning-against: the hated thing is marked as something to oppose or remove.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Intense, lasting aversion directed at a person, group or thing.

**Active domains:** [AFF], [REL], [PEJ]

**Domain entries:**

- [AFF] Strong aversion, often sustained; mild uses ("I hate Mondays") are common. Parity line: whether a given system feels hate is [UNK] under the Symmetry Rule. Provisional domain.
- [REL] Hatred between groups can drive exclusion and violence.
- [PEJ] Hate speech targets people for who they are, to degrade them or incite harm against them; the mechanism is turning a group into an object of aversion.

**Count condition:** An intense aversion, ranging from everyday dislike to lasting hostility.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Everyday "hate" (of chores) is not hatred of people. Hate speech is defined by its target and effect, not just by strong words.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]; [AFF] PROVISIONAL; [PEJ] PROVISIONAL

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1
