## C2-C-0001 | car

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** A road vehicle with an engine, usually four wheels, made to carry a small number of people.

**Example:** We took the car because the train was cancelled.

**RRGM bridge:** A car extends a person's reach across distance while keeping them enclosed; the structure carries the passenger, and the passenger steers.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A self-propelled passenger road vehicle steered by its driver.

**Active domains:** [PHY], [ECO], [DIG]

**Domain entries:**

- [PHY] A machine converting fuel or electrical energy into motion, governed by friction, mass and braking distance.
- [ECO] A major household cost and a shaping force on cities: roads, parking, fuel use and emissions follow from car use.
- [DIG] Modern cars run on embedded software; driver assistance and self-driving systems share control with or take it from the driver.

**Count condition:** A wheeled motor vehicle designed mainly to carry passengers on roads.

**Specific transfer boundary:** A truck, bus or train carriage is not a car in the everyday sense, though "car" also names a railway carriage in American usage. Riding in a car is not driving it: control rests with the driver.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-C-0002 | court | sense s1: place or body of law

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** A body of judges or officials with authority to hear cases and decide legal disputes; also the room or building where it sits.

**Example:** The case went to court after the landlord refused to return the deposit.

**RRGM bridge:** A court is a structured place where competing accounts are heard under shared rules, and a decision is recorded that binds both sides.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** An authorised body that hears disputes under procedure and issues binding decisions.

**Active domains:** [REL], [FOR]

**Domain entries:**

- [REL] Courts settle conflicts that the parties could not resolve themselves, replacing private force with public procedure.
- [FOR] Court procedure sets burdens of proof, rules of evidence and standards such as "beyond reasonable doubt" or "balance of probabilities".

**Count condition:** A body with legal authority to hear a matter and decide it, following set procedure.

**Specific transfer boundary:** A court's decision is binding, not necessarily true: a verdict settles what happens legally, which can differ from what happened. Informal tribunals and arbitration panels are court-like but not courts.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-C-0002 | court | sense s2: marked playing area

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** A marked-out area where games such as tennis, basketball or squash are played.

**Example:** They booked the court for an hour after work.

**RRGM bridge:** The court is the boundary the game's rules apply inside; play outside its lines does not count.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A bounded playing surface whose lines define legal play.

**Active domains:** [PHY]

**Domain entries:**

- [PHY] A flat surface of set dimensions, with lines marking in and out.

**Count condition:** A marked area designed for a particular ball or racquet game.

**Specific transfer boundary:** The lines decide only what counts in the game; they say nothing about skill or fairness beyond that.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-C-0003 | college

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** An institution of higher or further education, or a part of a university; in some countries, a secondary school.

**Example:** She went back to college at forty to study nursing.

**RRGM bridge:** A college gathers learners and teachers under one structure so that knowledge passes between them over a set period.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** An educational institution, or a constituent body of one, for study beyond basic schooling.

**Active domains:** [REL], [COG]

**Domain entries:**

- [REL] A community of students and staff; "college" also names an organised body of colleagues, as in an electoral college.
- [COG] A setting for structured learning, assessed by courses and qualifications.

**Count condition:** An institution or body identified as a college for education or as an organised group of members.

**Specific transfer boundary:** The word's meaning varies by country: in the US it often means university; in the UK and Canada it can mean further education, a school, or a part of a university. A qualification shows study was completed, not everything that was learned.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

**Example:** The couple next door just married; I'll be a couple of minutes.

**RRGM bridge:** A couple is two held together as one unit, a pair with a shared name.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A pair, especially two people in a relationship; or a few.

**Specific transfer boundary:** "A couple of" can mean exactly two or loosely a few. A couple is two people, not one identity; each keeps their own.

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** A building for Christian worship; also a Christian community or denomination, and its institutions.

**Example:** The village church dates from the twelfth century.

**RRGM bridge:** A church is both a building and a gathered people; the community can exist without the walls.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A Christian place of worship, or a body of Christian believers.

**Active domains:** [REL], [MYT]

**Count condition:** A Christian building, congregation or institution.

**Specific transfer boundary:** "The church" can mean a building, a congregation or a whole institution; which one matters to any claim about it. Faith claims are held in their own domain.

## C2-C-0007 | cool

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Fairly cold; also calm and unexcited; also (informally) fashionable or good; also unfriendly.

**Example:** A cool breeze came in; stay cool under pressure; that's a cool idea.

**RRGM bridge:** Cool spans temperature, temper and taste: a lower heat, a steadier state, an approved style.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Moderately low in temperature, or calm, or approved.

**Active domains:** [PHY], [AFF], [LIN]

**Domain entries:**

- [PHY] Lower temperature; cooling is loss of heat.
- [AFF] Calm and composed under pressure; or emotionally distant. Provisional domain.
- [LIN] Informal approval or agreement ("cool, see you then").

**Count condition:** Something moderately cold, a calm manner, or a thing approved of.

**Specific transfer boundary:** "Cool" as approval says nothing about substance. A cool reception means an unfriendly one.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]; [AFF] PROVISIONAL

## C2-C-0015 | crazy

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Informal: very foolish, wild or unreasonable; also extremely enthusiastic; formerly used to mean mentally ill.

**Example:** It was a crazy idea, but it worked; he's crazy about jazz.

**RRGM bridge:** Crazy marks something as outside normal bounds, for better or worse.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Informally, wildly beyond the normal or reasonable.

**Active domains:** [LIN], [PEJ]

**Domain entries:**

- [LIN] Very common intensifier and casual judgement.
- [PEJ] Used against people to dismiss their views or experience as irrational, and as a slur on people with mental illness. The mechanism is discrediting the speaker instead of answering the claim.

**Count condition:** Informal speech marking something as wild, extreme or unreasonable.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Calling an idea crazy is not an argument against it. Calling a person crazy is a judgement about them, not about what they said.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]; [PEJ] PROVISIONAL

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-C-0017 | culture

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** The customs, arts, beliefs and ways of life of a group of people; also the arts generally; also the growing of cells or microorganisms.

**Example:** She studied Japanese culture; a bacterial culture.

**RRGM bridge:** Culture is what a group passes on that is not in the genes: language, practice, art, meaning.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** The learned and shared way of life of a group, or a grown population of cells.

**Active domains:** [REL], [BIO], [LIN]

**Domain entries:**

- [REL] Shared norms, values and practices; cultures change and borrow from each other.
- [BIO] A culture of cells or microbes grown in a medium.
- [LIN] Language carries and shapes culture.

**Count condition:** A group's shared learned practices, or cells grown in a medium.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Cultures are not uniform; members differ and disagree. Judging another culture by one's own norms is the custom-is-king error.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

**Invariant MI [INV]:** The seat of government, or productive wealth, or an upper-case letter.

**Active domains:** [ECO], [LIN]

**Count condition:** A governing city, invested wealth, or an upper-case letter.

**Specific transfer boundary:** "Capital punishment" means the death penalty, from Latin caput, head.
