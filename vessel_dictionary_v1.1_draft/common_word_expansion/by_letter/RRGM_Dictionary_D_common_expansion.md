## C2-D-0001 | due

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Expected or required at a certain time; owed; or (with "to") caused by.

**Example:** The rent is due on the first; the delay was due to snow.

**RRGM bridge:** Due links a thing to a time or a cause it is answerable to: a payment to its date, an outcome to what produced it.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Owed or expected by a set point, or attributable to a stated cause.

**Active domains:** [ECO], [FOR], [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [ECO] A due payment is an obligation with a date; once past it, it is overdue and may carry penalties.
- [FOR] "Due to" states a causal attribution, which can be checked against alternatives.
- [REL] "Giving someone their due" means granting what they have fairly earned, including credit.

**Count condition:** A thing with a set time it is expected or owed by, or an outcome with an identified cause.

**Specific transfer boundary:** "Due to" asserts cause, not just sequence; that a delay followed snow does not prove the snow caused it. Being due is not the same as being paid or done.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-D-0002 | development

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** The process of growing, changing or becoming more advanced; also a new event, or land and buildings that have been built or improved.

**Example:** The child's language development was quick; there's been a development in the case.

**RRGM bridge:** Development is change with a direction: each stage builds on the last, so the earlier forms are carried into the later ones rather than erased.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Change over time in which later states build on and extend earlier ones.

**Active domains:** [BIO], [COG], [ECO], [DIG]

**Domain entries:**

- [BIO] Growth and maturation of an organism from conception through adulthood.
- [COG] Developmental stages in thinking, language and social understanding.
- [ECO] Economic development; and property development, the building of homes or offices on land.
- [DIG] Software development: designing, writing, testing and maintaining programs.

**Count condition:** A process of change in which successive states build on earlier ones, or an event that changes a situation.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Development is not always improvement; a situation can develop for the worse. Calling something "developing" assumes a direction that should be stated, not taken for granted.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-D-0003 | department

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** A division of a large organisation, such as a government, university, shop or company, dealing with a particular area.

**Example:** She works in the sales department.

**RRGM bridge:** A department is a section of a larger structure, given its own remit so the whole can divide its work.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A specialised division of an organisation.

**Active domains:** [ECO], [REL]

**Domain entries:**

- [ECO] Departments split budgets, staff and responsibility within an organisation.
- [REL] Department boundaries shape who talks to whom, and can become barriers.

**Count condition:** A named division with its own area of responsibility within an organisation.

**Specific transfer boundary:** A matter falling between departments can end up owned by none; "not our department" is a common way responsibility goes missing.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-D-0004 | director

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** A person who manages an organisation or department; also a member of a company's board; also the person who directs a film or play.

**Example:** The film's director cast unknown actors.

**RRGM bridge:** A director shapes where a group's effort goes, holding the overall view the parts work toward.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A person responsible for directing an organisation, activity or production.

**Active domains:** [REL], [ECO]

**Domain entries:**

- [REL] Directors hold authority and accountability, to boards, shareholders or the public.
- [ECO] Company directors have legal duties to the company.

**Count condition:** A person holding a directing role over an organisation, department or production.

**Specific transfer boundary:** A film's director shapes the film but does not make it alone; credit is shared across many roles.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1
