## C2-M-0001 | maybe

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Perhaps; possibly; used to show uncertainty or to make a suggestion softer.

**Example:** Maybe we should leave early to beat the traffic.

**RRGM bridge:** Maybe holds a possibility open without committing to it; it marks a claim as live but unweighed.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Marks a proposition as possible but not asserted.

**Active domains:** [LIN], [COG]

**Domain entries:**

- [LIN] Hedging adverb; also softens requests and suggestions.
- [COG] Expresses uncertainty without a stated probability.

**Count condition:** A proposition the speaker holds as possible but does not assert.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Maybe is not a probability; it could mean anything from unlikely to probable. Used as a polite refusal ("maybe another time"), it may mean no.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-M-0002 | million

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** The number 1,000,000; a thousand thousand; informally, a very large number.

**Example:** The city has about two million people.

**RRGM bridge:** A million is a count so large that it cannot be seen at once, only measured.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** The cardinal number ten to the sixth power.

**Active domains:** [FOR], [ECO]

**Domain entries:**

- [FOR] 10^6. A unit of scale in counting, money and measurement.
- [ECO] Common scale for budgets, populations and sales.

**Count condition:** A quantity equal to one thousand thousand, or used loosely for "very many".

**Specific transfer boundary:** In everyday speech "a million" is often exaggeration ("a million times"), not a count. Large numbers are easy to misjudge; comparisons should keep the same scale.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-M-0003 | market | sense s1: place or system of trade

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** A place where goods are bought and sold; also the whole system of buying and selling a kind of goods, or the demand for them.

**Example:** There's a farmers' market every Saturday; the market for used cars is strong.

**RRGM bridge:** A market is where offers meet demand and a price forms between them.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** A setting, physical or abstract, where buyers and sellers exchange and prices are set.

**Active domains:** [ECO], [REL], [DIG]

**Domain entries:**

- [ECO] Supply, demand and competition set prices; markets can fail, through monopoly, missing information or costs pushed onto others.
- [REL] Markets rely on trust and rules about fair dealing; most are regulated.
- [DIG] Online marketplaces and app stores, where a platform controls access and takes a share.

**Count condition:** Buyers and sellers able to exchange, with prices that respond to them.

**Specific transfer boundary:** A market price is what something sells for, not what it is worth in every sense. "The market decided" names an outcome of many choices; it does not mean no one decided.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-M-0003 | market | sense s2: to promote for sale

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** To promote and sell a product or service, especially through advertising.

**Example:** They marketed the game mainly to teenagers.

**RRGM bridge:** To market is to shape how a thing is seen so that people will want it.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** To present and promote something in order to sell it.

**Active domains:** [ECO], [COG]

**Domain entries:**

- [ECO] Marketing covers pricing, packaging, advertising and targeting particular buyers.
- [COG] Marketing works through attention, framing and association.

**Count condition:** A product or service and an effort to promote it to buyers.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Being well marketed is not the same as being good; the promotion and the product are judged separately.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-M-0004 | music

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Sounds organised in time, through rhythm, melody, harmony or timbre, made for expression or enjoyment; also the art of making them.

**Example:** She plays music every evening after work.

**RRGM bridge:** Music is pattern in sound that listeners can follow and anticipate, so that its turns can satisfy or surprise.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** Intentionally organised sound, structured in time, made or received as expression.

**Active domains:** [PHY], [COG], [AFF], [REL], [MYT]

**Domain entries:**

- [PHY] Vibration in air at frequencies and intervals; harmony follows ratios between frequencies.
- [COG] Listeners track rhythm and melody and form expectations, which music then fulfils or breaks.
- [AFF] Music can move listeners, evoking strong feelings. Parity line: whether a given system experiences music is [UNK] under the Symmetry Rule. Provisional domain.
- [REL] Shared music marks communities, rituals and identities.
- [MYT] Music of the spheres: the old idea that the cosmos is ordered by harmonic ratios.

**Count condition:** Sound organised in time by someone, or perceived by someone as organised.

**Specific transfer boundary:** What counts as music varies across cultures; a pattern being unfamiliar does not make it noise. Music can be generated by machines without settling whether the machine experiences it.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]; [AFF] PROVISIONAL

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-M-0005 | myself

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** Reflexive and emphatic form of I or me.

**Example:** I made it myself; I hurt myself on the step.

**RRGM bridge:** Myself bends the action back onto the speaker, who is both actor and the one acted upon.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** The speaker, as object of their own action or for emphasis.

**Active domains:** [LIN], [COG]

**Domain entries:**

- [LIN] Reflexive pronoun ("I cut myself") and intensifier ("I did it myself").
- [COG] The grammar of self-reference: the speaker naming themselves as both subject and object.

**Count condition:** A sentence where the speaker refers back to themselves.

**Specific transfer boundary:** Using "myself" in place of "I" or "me" ("contact myself") is common but often judged nonstandard. Self-reference in grammar does not settle questions about the self.

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1

## C2-M-0006 | march | sense s1: walk in step or protest

Discovery route: frequency-gap (route only, not a tag)

**Common meaning:** To walk with regular steps, as soldiers do; also to walk in a group as a public protest; and a piece of music for marching.

**Example:** Thousands marched to the parliament to demand change.

**RRGM bridge:** Marching aligns many walkers into one rhythm, so a crowd moves and is seen as a single body.

**Invariant MI [INV]:** To walk in a regulated, collective, purposeful way, or a public procession of that kind.

**Active domains:** [REL], [PHY]

**Domain entries:**

- [REL] A protest march makes a group's demand visible in public space.
- [PHY] Regular step; soldiers break step on bridges because rhythmic loading can build resonance.

**Count condition:** A group or individual walking in regulated step or in organised procession.

**Specific transfer boundary:** The size of a march shows how many turned out, not whether their demand is right. "March" as a verb is not the month March (a proper noun).

**Epistemic status:** [DEF]

Kind: discovery · prior exposure: none · record v1
