@ C2-C-0001 | car
cm: A road vehicle with an engine, usually four wheels, made to carry a small number of people.
ex: We took the car because the train was cancelled.
br: A car extends a person's reach across distance while keeping them enclosed; the structure carries the passenger, and the passenger steers.
inv: A self-propelled passenger road vehicle steered by its driver.
[PHY] A machine converting fuel or electrical energy into motion, governed by friction, mass and braking distance.
[ECO] A major household cost and a shaping force on cities: roads, parking, fuel use and emissions follow from car use.
[DIG] Modern cars run on embedded software; driver assistance and self-driving systems share control with or take it from the driver.
cc: A wheeled motor vehicle designed mainly to carry passengers on roads.
tb: A truck, bus or train carriage is not a car in the everyday sense, though "car" also names a railway carriage in American usage. Riding in a car is not driving it: control rests with the driver.

@ C2-C-0002 | court | s1 | place or body of law
cm: A body of judges or officials with authority to hear cases and decide legal disputes; also the room or building where it sits.
ex: The case went to court after the landlord refused to return the deposit.
br: A court is a structured place where competing accounts are heard under shared rules, and a decision is recorded that binds both sides.
inv: An authorised body that hears disputes under procedure and issues binding decisions.
[REL] Courts settle conflicts that the parties could not resolve themselves, replacing private force with public procedure.
[FOR] Court procedure sets burdens of proof, rules of evidence and standards such as "beyond reasonable doubt" or "balance of probabilities".
cc: A body with legal authority to hear a matter and decide it, following set procedure.
tb: A court's decision is binding, not necessarily true: a verdict settles what happens legally, which can differ from what happened. Informal tribunals and arbitration panels are court-like but not courts.

@ C2-C-0002 | court | s2 | marked playing area
cm: A marked-out area where games such as tennis, basketball or squash are played.
ex: They booked the court for an hour after work.
br: The court is the boundary the game's rules apply inside; play outside its lines does not count.
inv: A bounded playing surface whose lines define legal play.
[PHY] A flat surface of set dimensions, with lines marking in and out.
cc: A marked area designed for a particular ball or racquet game.
tb: The lines decide only what counts in the game; they say nothing about skill or fairness beyond that.

@ C2-C-0003 | college
cm: An institution of higher or further education, or a part of a university; in some countries, a secondary school.
ex: She went back to college at forty to study nursing.
br: A college gathers learners and teachers under one structure so that knowledge passes between them over a set period.
inv: An educational institution, or a constituent body of one, for study beyond basic schooling.
[REL] A community of students and staff; "college" also names an organised body of colleagues, as in an electoral college.
[COG] A setting for structured learning, assessed by courses and qualifications.
cc: An institution or body identified as a college for education or as an organised group of members.
tb: The word's meaning varies by country: in the US it often means university; in the UK and Canada it can mean further education, a school, or a part of a university. A qualification shows study was completed, not everything that was learned.
