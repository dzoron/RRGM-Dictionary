@ C2-T-0001 | those
cm: Plural of that: referring to people or things at a distance, or already mentioned.
ex: Those shoes by the door are mine.
br: Those points outward to a group set apart from the speaker, in space, time or discussion.
inv: A plural demonstrative pointing to referents distant from the speaker or previously identified.
[LIN] Demonstrative determiner and pronoun, plural of "that"; contrasts with "these".
cc: A plural referent that is distant or already introduced.
tb: "Those people" can be used to distance or other a group; the pointing word can carry a judgement.

@ C2-T-0002 | today
cm: On or during this present day; also in the present period.
ex: I have to finish the report today; young people today use phones for everything.
br: Today is the present day, the one unit of time in which action can still be taken.
inv: The current day, or the present era.
[LIN] Deictic: what "today" refers to depends on when it is said.
[DIG] For a system without a clock, "today" has to be supplied from outside; it cannot be inferred from training alone.
cc: The day on which the speaker speaks, or the present age.
tb: "Today" in a written record means the day it was written, not the day it is read. Statements about "people today" are generalisations.

@ C2-T-0003 | though
cm: Despite the fact that; however; nevertheless.
ex: Though it was raining, we went for a walk.
br: Though holds two facts together, one of which might have prevented the other, and lets both stand.
inv: Introduces a concession: a fact that runs against the main claim without cancelling it.
[LIN] Conjunction ("though it rained") and adverb ("it was good, though").
[FOR] Concession: both clauses are asserted; the contrast is between expectation and outcome.
cc: Two statements in tension, both held true.
tb: A concession does not weaken the main claim; it admits a fact that might have been expected to.
