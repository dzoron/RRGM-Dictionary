@ C2-B-0001 | business | s1 | commercial activity or firm
cm: The activity of making, buying or selling goods or services for money; also a company or firm that does this.
ex: Her bakery business grew from one oven to three shops.
br: A business is a structure built to keep an exchange going: value goes out, payment comes back, and the loop has to close often enough for the structure to persist.
inv: An organised activity or entity whose continuation depends on exchanging goods or services for payment.
[ECO] A firm or trade carried on for profit or at least for revenue; measured by sales, costs, margins and survival over time.
[REL] Business relations are governed by contract and reputation rather than kinship; trust is built through repeated fair exchange.
[DIG] Online businesses sell through platforms, where the platform's rules and fees become part of the exchange.
cc: An activity or entity that offers goods or services in exchange for payment on a continuing basis.
tb: Not every exchange is a business: a one-off sale between friends is a transaction, not a business. A charity can run business-like operations without being a business in the profit sense.

@ C2-B-0001 | business | s2 | one's concern or affair
cm: A matter that concerns someone, or that someone has a right to be involved in.
ex: Where I spend my weekend is none of their business.
br: Business in this sense draws a boundary: it says whose say a matter falls under.
inv: A matter assigned to someone's legitimate concern or authority.
[REL] Used to claim or deny standing in a matter: "that's my business" asserts a boundary; "none of your business" refuses another's claim to involvement.
cc: A matter and a person whose standing to be involved is being asserted or denied.
tb: Saying something is not someone's business does not settle whether it affects them; a matter can be private and still have consequences for others.
