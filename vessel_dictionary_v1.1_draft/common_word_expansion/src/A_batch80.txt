@ C2-A-0001 | actually
cm: In fact; as things really are, often in contrast to what was expected, assumed or said before.
ex: I thought the shop closed at six, but it actually closes at eight.
br: Actually marks a correction arriving: the speaker sets a checked state of affairs against an earlier belief and lets the checked one stand.
inv: Asserts that what follows is the real case, set against an expectation or earlier claim that it overrides.
[LIN] A stance adverb: it signals contrast with an assumption and is often used to soften a correction or disagreement in conversation.
[COG] Marks belief revision in speech: the prior expectation is named or implied, and the update is offered as the outcome of a check.
cc: There is an earlier expectation, claim or assumption, stated or implied, that the actually-clause contradicts or refines.
tb: Saying "actually" does not make a claim checked; it signals the speaker's confidence in the correction, which still needs its own evidence. As a filler ("actually, yeah") it carries little more than emphasis.
